# Commercial product blueprint

Position as a Finance Architecture and Accounting Events Workbench: a governed design, mapping and assurance
accelerator for new product onboarding, ERP/subledger transformation and reporting change. Market measured reduction
in design/rework and improved traceability once demonstrated. Avoid promising autonomous regulatory compliance or
replacing professional accounting judgment.

## Sellable offers and acceptance

| Offer | Fixed boundary | Evidence delivered | Commercial basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design diagnostic | One product family, entity/basis footprint and selected platform estate | Gap register, architecture options, event coverage and prioritized implementation backlog | Fixed scope/fee after intake |
| Paid design-partner pilot | Residential mortgages; agreed lifecycle cases; one hub/ERP/EPM path; masked data | Policy-reviewed golden cases, offline/sandbox simulations, reconciliation, regulatory/EPM lineage and measured design effort | Milestone payments; acceptance criteria agreed before build |
| Enterprise subscription | Versioned policy/event packs, metadata/rule change governance, regression library and controlled workbench | Tenant-isolated operations, support, upgrades and agreed SLAs | Annual licence + supported modules/entities/product families; price validated through sales discovery |
| Implementation / managed change | Client-specific adapters, migration, release and operating model | Certified-by-client deployment evidence and ongoing change controls | Separate SOW/service contract, not hidden in licence |

Differentiation must rest on reusable event ontology, policy decision coverage, traceable mappings, approved golden
cases, deterministic validation and platform change knowledge. A prompt alone is easy to copy and provides a weak
defensible product boundary. Keep generic IP separate from client policies/data and confirm ownership, employer
obligations and rights to distribute content before commercial sale.

## Proposed 90-day stage-gated roadmap

| Window | Build | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1-15 | Scope and rights, paid design partner, policy owners, deployment boundary, source inventory and target outcomes | Signed scope; usable masked data; named policy/security/architecture owners |
| Days 16-35 | Mortgage event ontology, policies, COA/ledger/hub/EPM crosswalks and approved expected cases | All agreed cases have policies/owners; missing cases explicitly blocked |
| Days 36-60 | One sandbox adapter path, durable rules/evidence store, strict APIs, deterministic checks and data isolation | Sandbox accounting and reconciliations pass; no unresolved critical security issues |
| Days 61-75 | UAT, failure/replay/scale testing, model-risk applicability review, bank supplier diligence and measured business case | Joint accounting/technology/control acceptance for agreed pilot scope |
| Days 76-90 | Contracting, support/incident/exit arrangements, release pack and controlled rollout decision | Bank-approved go/no-go; evidence-backed case study only with permission |

This is a proposed sequence, not a commitment that a large bank can complete procurement/production approval within
90 days. Elapsed delivery depends on access, approvals, integrations and contract scope.

## Value model and pricing discipline

Use observed counts/hours/rework before and after with comparable case complexity. Example only: 400 annual
design/review cases x 8 hours = 3,200 baseline hours. A hypothetical 30% reduction releases 960 hours; at an
illustrative fully loaded CAD 200/hour this equals CAD 192,000 capacity value. It is not cash savings unless costs are
actually removed/avoided. If incremental annual licence+operations cost CAD 120,000, illustrative net capacity value is
CAD 72,000/year before one-time implementation. For CAD 100,000 one-time implementation, simple payback on that net
value is about 16.7 months; first-year net value is negative CAD 28,000. Replace every input with client evidence. No
amount here is a market quote, benchmark or recommended bank price.

Price from client value, cost-to-serve, scope, risk, commercial discovery and competitive alternatives. Track gross
margin after SMEs, support, cloud/model cost, content maintenance, assurance and insurance. Separate one-time
implementation from recurring margin. Validate willingness to pay with paid pilots rather than asserting market
demand.

## Bank procurement and operating evidence

Provide company/IP ownership, secure architecture/data flows, subprocessor list, data use/retention/deletion, incident
notification, business continuity/restore evidence, threat model and penetration-test findings, access/SoD,
auditability, vulnerability/dependency management, exit/data portability, insurance/liability and actual service
levels. Offer client-controlled deployment where required; data residency, access and contractual terms must reflect
that bank's obligations rather than a blanket claim that all Canadian banking data must remain in Canada.

Assess OSFI B-10 supplier risk and B-13 technology/cyber expectations with the bank; they are
institution-accountability frameworks, not a vendor certification. BCBS 239 risk-data principles can inform lineage
and data controls; determine institutional scope. Assess model-risk applicability, especially if outputs influence
financial/risk decisions. Revised E-23 takes effect May 1, 2027, as of the review date November 1, 2026; do not
describe it as already effective. Do not claim SOC 2/ISO certification, regulatory approval or vendor partnership
unless independently evidenced. Sources S21-S23, S30.
