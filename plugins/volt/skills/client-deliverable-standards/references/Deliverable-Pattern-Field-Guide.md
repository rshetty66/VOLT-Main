# VOLT client deliverable pattern field guide

Choose the question the recipient must answer, not the consulting firm whose report you liked. VOLT uses the reference to suggest an original storyline, a decision exhibit and the level of analysis the decision warrants. It does not treat the example as client evidence or apply its figures to another engagement.

## Choose the right deliverable

| When the client asks… | Ask VOLT for… | The exhibit to look for |
|---|---|---|
| What can we afford over the planning horizon? | A five-year financial plan | Baseline and action trajectories with a timed register of levers |
| What exactly are we buying or changing? | A statement of work or change order | Deliverables, exclusions, acceptance, timing and fee changes |
| Did the promised benefits actually materialize? | A benefits realization review | Claim-to-actual reconciliation, including unsupported and disputed benefits |
| How should central teams serve the front line? | A support-function redesign | A before-and-after handoff with changed decision rights |
| What is driving expenditure growth? | A line-by-line expenditure review | A spending bridge separating growth drivers and interventions |
| What deserves deeper financial diligence? | A fiscal performance diagnostic | A screened opportunity register, not a committed savings number |
| What discretion do we have over this service? | Committee service profiles | A comparable card per service: mandate, level, cost and consequences |
| Which services warrant a portfolio decision? | A core service portfolio review | Mandate and service-level filters applied consistently across the portfolio |
| Which structural alternative works best? | A detailed options appraisal | Options compared on a common baseline, with transition costs and sensitivities |
| What does the board need in order to choose? | A board options summary | A short comparison using the same option names and figures as the appraisal |
| Where should work sit, and what should we fund first? | A target operating model and investment portfolio | Decision rights and shared/local work mapped to individual business cases |
| How will the bidder deliver against our requirements? | An RFP proposal response | A requirement-to-response matrix linked to work packages and acceptance |
| Which sourcing waves should we authorize? | A procurement business case | Addressable spend, phasing, implementation cost and net benefit |
| What is the near-term impact of a sudden shock? | A rapid financial-impact assessment | Observed change, baseline exposure and conditional recovery paths |
| Why has the forecast changed between releases? | A forecast vintage and methodology guide | A release matrix distinguishing new actuals, revised assumptions and coverage changes |
| How could demand and revenue evolve over several years? | A multi-year demand and revenue outlook | Actuals and scenarios on a common basis, with volume separated from yield |

## Three prompts to try

**Board decision.** “Prepare a board options summary for our Canadian insurer. Compare keeping the current close model with centralizing close operations. Use only the attached client facts. State the decision, the strongest argument against the preferred option, and what would change the recommendation. Keep the detail in an appendix.”

**Independent challenge.** “Validate these savings for the controller. Use the approved business case and ledger extracts. Distinguish realized cost reduction, cost avoidance, timing and unsupported amounts. Give management a one-page readout with the unresolved decisions. Do not assume the claimed benefits were achieved.”

**Forecast story.** “Create a multi-year demand and revenue outlook for our fee business. Use the volume and fee data I supplied. Show where actuals end, which assumptions distinguish the scenarios and which trigger would make us change the plan. If data are insufficient to quantify a scenario, explain the missing input rather than inventing a range.”

## Set the depth before generation

Name the recipient, decision, output format, time horizon, and available client data. Ask for one decision exhibit in a short brief; request methods, sensitivities and detailed schedules only when the audience needs them. To explore without committing to a format, ask: “Suggest three deliverable ideas for this decision, identify who will use each, and recommend the smallest one that will work.”

The source corpus comprises the Harris County plan and change order; NYCHA support redesign and the independent NYC Comptroller audit; Ontario, Manitoba and Toronto reviews; Nottinghamshire appraisal and summary; Iowa operating model, RFP response and sourcing case; and three MTA shock, forecast-methodology and demand-outlook publications. Source filenames and PDF page locators are retained in the internal pattern library, not in ordinary client output.
