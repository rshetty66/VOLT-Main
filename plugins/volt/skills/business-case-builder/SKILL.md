# Business Case Builder

Structured framework for building business cases that get approved. Combines financial rigor with clear executive communication.

## When to Use

- Requesting budget for a new initiative
- Proposing a tool, vendor, or technology investment
- Justifying headcount or team expansion
- Making build vs. buy recommendations
- Any decision requiring executive/finance approval

## Core Workflow

```
1. FRAME — What decision are we enabling?
      |
2. QUANTIFY — What are the costs and benefits?
      |
3. COMPARE — What are the alternatives?
      |
4. RISK — What could go wrong?
      |
5. RECOMMEND — What should we do and why?
      |
OUTPUT: Executive-ready business case
```

## Step 1: FRAME the Decision

Before any numbers, clarify what we're actually deciding.

**Key questions:**
- What is the specific decision or approval being requested?
- What problem does this solve or opportunity does it capture?
- Why now? What's the cost of delay or inaction?
- Who are the stakeholders and what do they care about?

**Output:**
- Decision Requested: [Specific approval]
- Problem/Opportunity: [What this addresses]
- Strategic Alignment: [How this connects to priorities]
- Cost of Inaction: [What happens if we don't]

## Step 2: QUANTIFY Costs and Benefits

### Cost Categories
- **One-Time:** Purchase, implementation, training, migration
- **Ongoing:** Subscriptions, headcount (fully loaded 1.3-1.5x), infrastructure, support
- **Hidden:** Management attention, change management, productivity dip, vendor lock-in

### Benefit Categories
- **Hard (measurable):** Revenue increase, cost reduction, time savings, error reduction
- **Soft (real but harder):** Employee satisfaction, customer experience, strategic positioning

**Quantification:**
- Use ranges: conservative / expected / optimistic
- State assumptions explicitly
- Time savings: Hours x people x frequency x hourly cost
- Revenue: Pipeline x conversion x average deal size

## Step 3: COMPARE Alternatives

Every business case needs options including "do nothing":
1. Status quo — continue as-is
2. Proposed solution — recommendation
3. Alternative A — different approach
4. Alternative B — scaled-down/phased version

Assess each on: Total cost, benefits, time to value, risk level, strategic fit.

## Step 4: Calculate Key Metrics

- **ROI** = (Benefits - Costs) / Costs x 100%
- **Payback Period** = Total Investment / Annual Net Benefit
- **NPV** = Sum of discounted cash flows - initial investment (10-15% discount)
- **Break-Even** = Month when cumulative benefits exceed cumulative costs

## Step 5: ASSESS Risks

For each risk: Describe, rate likelihood (H/M/L), rate impact (H/M/L), define mitigation, identify early warnings.

Categories: Execution, Adoption, Technical, Vendor, Market, Financial.

Include: Contingency plan and kill criteria.

## Step 6: BUILD the Recommendation

**Executive Summary (lead with this):**
1. What we're recommending (one sentence)
2. Why it matters (problem/opportunity)
3. What it costs (total investment)
4. What we get (key benefits and ROI)
5. What we need (specific approval)

## Formats by Investment Size

- **Quick (<$50K):** 1 page — decision, cost/benefit table, ROI, top 3 risks, recommendation
- **Standard ($50K-$500K):** 2-3 pages — full workflow, detailed assumptions
- **Comprehensive (>$500K):** 5+ pages — scenarios, sensitivity, implementation plan, stakeholder analysis

## Tailoring by Audience

- **CFO:** Lead with numbers, sensitivity analysis, payback, hurdle rate
- **COO:** Efficiency gains, implementation feasibility, resource requirements
- **CEO/Board:** Strategic alignment, competitive implications, optionality
- **CTO:** Technical risk, integration complexity, build vs buy, tech debt
