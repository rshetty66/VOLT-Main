## 1. Scope and lease definition

IFRS 16 applies to leases of property, plant, equipment and other assets. Exclusions: intangible assets (IAS 38 policy choice), biological assets, minerals, exploration rights, service concessions (IFRIC 12).

**Lease definition (IFRS 16 9–11)**: a contract (or part) that conveys the **right to control the use of an identified asset** for a period in exchange for consideration. Control requires both:
- The customer obtains **substantially all of the economic benefits** from use of the asset; and
- The customer has the **right to direct use** of the asset (predetermined → relevant decisions that most affect economic benefits).

**Substitution rights**: if a supplier has a substantive substitution right (practically feasible, economically beneficial to substitute), the asset is not identified and there is no lease.

Reassess lease conclusion only on reassessment/modification events (IFRS 16 13, 44).

## 2. Separation and exemptions (5–8)

- Separate lease from non-lease components (account separately; allocate consideration per relative stand-alone prices). Practical expedient for **lessees** (not lessors): account for each lease component and its associated non-lease components as a single lease component.
- **Short-term lease exemption**: lease term ≤ 12 months and no purchase option. Apply per underlying class of asset; lease-by-lease election; disclose.
- **Low-value asset exemption**: value ~USD 5,000 or less when **new** (B8 lists personal computers, small office furniture, telephones — not leased-out again, except subleases of ROU assets... note the sublease exception). Applies to the underlying asset, per-lease election; not available to assets of **material value** to the entity.

## 3. Lease term (B34–B41)

Non-cancellable period + periods covered by renewal options the lessee is **reasonably certain** to exercise + periods covered by termination options the lessee is **reasonably certain not** to exercise. Termination penalties extend the lease if it is reasonably certain they will be paid.

**Reasonably certain** considers: contractual terms relative to market rates, significant leasehold improvements, termination penalties, importance of the asset to operations, options in other contracts, costs of replacing the asset, conditions around the option date. Lessee and lessor reassess if a significant event within the lessee's control changes the assessment (modifications also reassess term).

## 4. Lessee accounting — initial measurement (20–27)

Single on-balance-sheet model for lessees.

**Lease liability** = present value of lease payments at the **commencement date**, discounted at:
- The rate **implicit in the lease** if readily determinable; otherwise
- The lessee's **incremental borrowing rate** (IBR) — the rate the lessee would pay to borrow over a similar term, with similar security, to obtain an asset of similar value (B26–B31; entity-specific).

**Lease payments** (27):
- Fixed payments (including in-substance fixed), less lease incentives receivable;
- Variable lease payments that depend on an **index or a rate** (remeasured when the index/rate changes);
- Amounts expected to be payable under residual value guarantees;
- Exercise price of purchase options if **reasonably certain** to be exercised;
- Termination penalties if the lease term reflects expectation of exercising the termination option.

Excluded: variable payments not tied to an index/rate (expensed as incurred, by nature), taxes assessed on ownership (lessee may elect to include in lease payments).

**Right-of-use (ROU) asset** (24): lease liability + lease payments made at/before commencement (less incentives) + initial direct costs + estimated restoration/dismantling costs (IAS 37 provision). Subsequent: apply **cost model** (depreciation + IAS 36 impairment), or revaluation (IAS 16) / fair value (IAS 40) policy by asset class.

**Depreciation period**: shorter of useful life and lease term; **full useful life** if ownership transfers or purchase option reasonably certain.

## 5. Subsequent measurement and remeasurement (29–43)

- Interest on the liability accretes at the **discount rate locked at commencement** (29) unless remeasurement uses a revised rate.
- **Remeasure the liability** (and adjust ROU) when (39–42): index/rate-linked variable payments change; the lease term changes (reassessment of renewal/termination options); assessment of purchase-option exercise changes; residual value guarantees change.
- **Revised discount rate** applies only to term/purchase-option reassessments (IBR at revision date); index changes use the original rate.
- After modification or remeasurement events, the ROU asset is reduced by the amount of any previously recognised accumulated depreciation and impairment (36) — i.e., adjust remaining ROU, not restart.
- Impairment: apply IAS 36 to the ROU asset (cash-generating units as applicable).
- Rent holidays/COVID-type concessions: amendments introduce practical expedients for lessees (check for amendment in force for the period — e.g., May 2020 amendment permitting simplified accounting for rent concessions directly caused by COVID-19).

## 6. Lease modifications (44–46)

A modification increases scope (additional right of use) **and** the consideration increases by an amount commensurate with the stand-alone price → account as a **separate lease**; otherwise account for by:
1. Remeasuring the lease liability by discounting revised payments at the **revised discount rate** (revision-date rate);
2. Adjusting the ROU asset accordingly;
3. Any residual adjustment to the ROU is recognised in P&L.

Full/partial termination of a lease reduces ROU and liability (difference to P&L).

## 7. Lessor accounting — finance vs operating (61–66, B53–B63)

Classify at inception (reassess only on modification): a lease is a **finance lease** if it transfers substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership. Classification indicators:
- Transfer of ownership by end of term;
- Purchase option reasonably certain to be exercised;
- PV of lease payments amounts to **substantially all** of the asset's fair value (commonly ~90% as a benchmark indicator);
- Lease term covers the **major part** of the asset's economic life (commonly ~75% indicator);
- Asset is of such a specialised nature only the lessee can use without major modification;
- Plus loss compensation/secondary indicators (B59–B61).

**Finance lessor**:
- Derecognise underlying asset; recognise **net investment in the lease** (PV of lease payments + unguaranteed residual value, discounted at the rate implicit in the lease).
- Selling profit recognised at commencement (gross for manufacturer/dealer lessors, net of initial direct costs and market discounts).
- Finance income over the term at a constant periodic rate; variable payments not in the measurement are income as earned.
- Impairment: apply IFRS 9 to the net investment (net of unguaranteed residual value) — ECL.

**Operating lessor**: keep the asset; depreciate under the relevant standard; lease income on a straight-line or other systematic basis (B65–B66). Initial direct costs added to the carrying amount of the leased asset and expensed over the term (not for manufacturer/dealer lessors).

## 8. Sale and leaseback; subleases

**Sale and leaseback (98–103)**:
1. Apply IFRS 15 to determine whether the transfer of the asset qualifies as a sale.
2. If it **is a sale**: the seller-lessee measures the ROU asset at the proportion of the previous carrying amount that relates to the right of use retained; gain recognised only for rights **transferred** (gain on retained rights is deferred through the ROU measurement). The buyer-lessor accounts the purchase and lease under IFRS 16.
3. If it is **not a sale** (transfer fails IFRS 15): seller-lessee continues to recognise the asset and the proceeds are a financial liability (IFRS 9); buyer-lessor does not recognise the asset — recognises a financial asset.

**Subleases**: the intermediate lessor classifies the sublease by reference to the **ROU asset** (not the underlying asset) from the head lease. A sublease of an entire remaining head-lease term is effectively a transfer of the right of use (often finance-classified).

## 9. Presentation (IFRS 18) and disclosures

**IFRS 18 classification for lessees**: ROU asset depreciation and most interest cost in **operating**; interest on the lease liability and the **repayment of lease liability principal** in **financing**; variable lease payments not included in the liability in **operating** (by nature).

**Disclosures (IFRS 16 47–60)**: maturity analysis of lease liabilities; reconciliation of opening/closing carrying amounts of ROU assets by class; expense from short-term and low-value leases; variable lease payments not in the liability; total cash outflow; gains/losses on sale-and-leaseback; maturity analysis of lease receivables and unearned finance income for lessors; lessor classification and selling-profit disclosure for manufacturer/dealer lessors.
