## 1. Status and effective date

IFRS 18, issued April 2024, replaces **IAS 1** (Presentation of Financial Statements) and applies to **annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027** (early application permitted). It changes presentation and disclosure only — **no change to recognition or measurement**. Entities restate comparatives and report transition disclosures in the first IFRS 18 period. Related amendments: IAS 7 and IFRS 7 (supplier finance arrangements), IFRS 7 (disaggregation/disclosures alignment), IAS 34 (interim reporting), IAS 8 (definitions of accounting estimates — already effective 2023). IFRS 18 also incorporates the guidance from IFRS 5 on discontinued operations presentation categories.

## 2. Structure of the statement of profit or loss (IFRS 18 47–73, B58–B110)

Five defined categories; items classified by their **nature and role** in the entity's operations:

1. **Operating** — the default category: all income and expenses not classified elsewhere. Includes revenue, cost of sales, operating expenses, share of profit of associates and JVs (equity method), impairments of operating assets, depreciation/amortisation of operating assets, income tax refunds/charges (except... income tax is its own category — see 4).
2. **Investing** — income and expenses from investments in associates and JVs (equity method), cash and cash equivalents, investment property (except for certain... investment property measured under IAS 40 cost model remains operating), most investment financial assets, and gains/losses on disposal of investments.
3. **Financing** — interest expense on borrowings and lease liabilities, other financing liabilities (preference shares classified as liabilities, except those relating to customer financing), derivative liabilities hedging financing items, foreign exchange differences on financing items.
4. **Income tax** — income tax expense/income.
5. **Discontinued operations** — post-tax results of discontinued operations (after IFRS 5).

Classification is per item based on the entity's activities, not per account — the standard provides detailed requirements by item type (income, expenses, gains/losses by instrument), with specific rules for derivatives used for financing vs investing, gains/losses on derecognition, etc.

## 3. Entities with specified main business activities (B58–B110)

When the entity's main business activity is one of the specified types, different classification applies:

- **Banks and similar lenders**: interest income/expense and related impairment on loans/financial instruments used in providing financing to customers are classified in **operating**.
- **Insurers**: insurance revenue and insurance service expenses in **operating**; insurance finance income/expense and income from assets backing insurance liabilities in **investing**.
- **Asset managers**: income/expenses from managing assets are operating; gains/losses on investments related to fiduciary... (entity's own investments per general rules).
- **Customer-financing entities**: e.g., entities leasing to customers — income/expenses from assets used to finance customers are operating.

Main business activity is assessed with reference to observable evidence (revenue mix, business model, communication to investors, KPIs). An entity may be in more than one specified category with proportional rules.

## 4. Required subtotals and totals

The statement of profit or loss must present, at minimum:
- **Operating profit or loss** (subtotal of the operating category);
- **Profit or loss before financing and income tax** = operating + investing;
- **Profit or loss** (total).

Additional subtotals/aggregations are permitted if relevant to understanding and labeled clearly; they cannot obscure the required subtotals.

## 5. Management-defined performance measures (MPMs) (IFRS 18 120–132, B114–B116)

An MPM is a subtotal of income/expenses used in **public communications outside financial statements** (press releases, investor presentations) that communicates management's view of financial performance. Requirements:
- Disclose MPMs in a **single note** to the financial statements, with: the label and description; an explanation of why it provides useful information; a reconciliation to the most directly comparable IFRS subtotal/total, with income tax and NCI effects separately disclosed; and the nature and amounts of each reconciling item.
- Location: one note (may be near the front of the notes); aggregation consistent with the face presentation.
- MPMs are not IFRS measures; the note must state that.
- Changes to an MPM are treated as accounting-policy-like changes (disclose nature and effect).
- Fair value changes on hybrid contracts, impairment reversals through profit/loss, and fair-value-measurement-specific items are excluded from MPMs when they would inappropriately communicate performance.

## 6. Aggregation, disaggregation and expense classification

**Aggregation criteria**: items are aggregated only if they share characteristics (nature, role, measurement basis). Items with dissimilar characteristics are disaggregated. Totals and subtotals are "defined" items — the standard sets minimum line items by statement.

**Disaggregation in the notes**: certain items must be disaggregated in the notes, including (minimum list):
- Depreciation and amortisation of non-current assets;
- Employee benefits expense;
- Write-downs/reversals of inventories and impairment losses/reversals of non-current assets;
- Gains/losses on derecognition of non-current assets held for sale (IFRS 5);
- Net foreign exchange gains/losses;
- Gains/losses on fair value less costs of disposal measurements;
- Gains/losses on financial assets/liabilities measured at FVTPL (including derivatives);
- Impairment losses/reversals on financial assets (IFRS 9), gains/losses on derecognition of financial assets, and gains/losses on modifications of financial assets/liabilities not derecognised;
- Gains/losses on hedging instruments.

**Expense classification (nature vs function)**: entities classify expenses in the P&L by **nature or function** (as under IAS 1); if using the function (cost of sales) method, the nature of some expenses (D&A, employee benefits, inventory write-downs, impairment) must still be disclosed in the notes. IFRS 18 does not change the fundamental nature/function choice but tightens the disaggregation requirements.

## 7. Other statements and equity

- **Statement of financial position**: no wholesale category changes; assets/liabilities presented current vs non-current as current practice, with working-capital... (liquidity/maturity presentation moved from IAS 1). Equity: issued capital and reserves; disclosure of OCI by item, separated into items **that will be reclassified** to P&L and items that **will not be reclassified**.
- **Statement of changes in equity**: total comprehensive income, transactions with owners, and each class of reserves (including OCI components).
- **Statement of cash flows**: IFRS 18 moves some guidance from IAS 1/IAS 7; the operating/investing/financing categories in the cash flow statement generally align with the P&L categories.

## 8. Materiality and the notes

The IFRS 18 materiality model: information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably influence decisions. The notes are structured to support the face statements: accounting policies are still required (IAS 8) but policy information is materiality-focused; the notes must include a list of all statements presented and the basis of preparation; disclosures must be specific to the entity (no boilerplate).

## 9. Transition and practical implementation

- Restate comparatives for the previous period (and present a third statement of financial position if a retrospective policy change... generally not required for IFRS 18 adoption; check IFRS 18 transition clause).
- Transition disclosures: nature of the change, quantitative reconciliation of the P&L line items under IAS 1 vs IFRS 18 categories, and description of MPMs newly disclosed.
- Implementation workstreams: P&L mapping (chart of accounts to categories), MPM policy, subtotal design, disaggregation review of the notes, system/reporting changes, and investor communication alignment.

## 10. Interaction with IFRS 9 / 16 / 17

| Item | IFRS 18 category |
|---|---|
| Financial assets held for investing (incl. FVTPL, FVOCI debt under HTC&S) | Investing |
| Interest on amortised-cost assets used in customer financing | Operating (specified main activity) or investing |
| Lease liability interest and principal repayments (lessee) | Financing |
| ROU depreciation | Operating |
| Variable lease payments not in liability | Operating |
| Insurance revenue / insurance service expenses | Operating |
| Insurance finance income/expense; income from assets backing insurance liabilities | Investing |
| Own credit changes on FVTPL liabilities (OCI) | OCI (not category-specific) |
| IFRS 9 ECL impairment on financial assets | Follows the asset's category (operating or investing) |

IFRS 18 does not change the **measurement** of anything above — only where it is presented.
